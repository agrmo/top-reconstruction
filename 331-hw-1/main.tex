\section*{Problem 1.}
C.f. Peskin Ch. 9, Srednicky Ch. 44. We will calculate both (3.1) and (3.2) just to be thorough. For the c-number Gaussian integral,
\n{intf}{\int_{-\infty}^\infty}
\n{nih}{-\br{1}{2}}
\n{egh}{\br{1}{2}}
\n{dui}{(\det U)^{-1}}
\n{pin}{\Pi_{\alpha=1}^{\br{1}{2}n}}
\g{\intf \intf e^{\nih x^T A x} dx_1 ... dx_n &= \int e^{\nih y^T D y} dy_1 ... dy_n \\
&= \int dy_1...dy_n e^{\nih \sum lambda_i y_i^2}\\
\intertext{The $n$ integrals can be separated.}
&= \Pi_i \int dy_i e^{\nih \lambda_i y_i^2} \\
\intertext{The integrals are each Gaussian integrals.}
&= \Pi_i (\br{2\pi}{\lambda_i})^{1/2}
\intertext{Using $\Pi_i \lambda_i = \det D = \det(C^{-1} A C) = \det A$ we have the answer for the real valued case.}
&= (\br{(2\pi)^n}{\det A})^{1/2}}
Now for the Grassman-Gauss integral. Let $M$ be a complex antisymmetric matrix.
\g{\psi_i = U_{ij} \psi_j'}
\g{\int d^d\psi e^{\egh \psi^T M \psi} &= \dui \pin d^2 \psi_\alpha e^{\egh \psi^T M_\alpha \psi}\intertext{Use $\int d^2 \psi_\alpha e^{\egh \psi^T M_\alpha \psi = m_\alpha}$}
&= \dui \pin m_\alpha\\
&= \boxed{(\det M)^{\br{1}{2}}}}
\section*{Problem 2.}
\n{proded}{\prod_{n=1,3,...}^\infty}
\n{prodez}{\prod_{n=1,2,...}^\infty}
\n{summez}{\sum_{n=1}^\infty}
C.f. Tong's SUSY notes. We have a couple approaches which have varying degrees of rigor.
\g{\zeich{nz8xcn}\prod_{\br{1}{2},\br{3}{2},...}^\infty \br{n}{\beta} = \br{1}{2\beta}\cdot\br{3}{2\beta}\cdot\br{5}{2\beta}... = \br{\proded n}{\proded 2\beta}=\br{\prodez n}{\prodez 2\beta}}
Here we claim that a ratio of products over every other integer is the same as a ratio of products over all integers. With that in mind the two products can be regularized with the zeta function as usual. The first product is,
\n{sneu}{\sum_{n=1}^\infty}
\n{pneu}{\prod_{n=1}^\infty}
\g{\zeta(s)=\sum \br{1}{n^s}}
\g{\dd{x} a^x = a^x \log a}
\g{\dd{s}(n^{-s})=-n^{-s}\log n}
\g{\zeta(s)'=-\sneu \br{\log n}{n^s}}
\g{\zeta(0)'=-\sneu \log{n} = -\br{1}{2}\log(2\pi)}
So the infinite product of $n$ is
\g{\pneu n = e^{\sneu \log n}=e^{\br{1}{2}\log(2\pi)}=(2\pi)^{1/2}}
The second product is,
\g{a=e^{\log a}}
\g{\prodez a = e^{\summez \log a} = e^{\log a + \log a + ...} = e^{(1+1+...)\log a} = e^{-\br{1}{2}\log a} = \br{1}{\sqrt{a}}}
And so the regularized product \gzeich{nz8xcn} is
\g{\prod_{\br{1}{2},\br{3}{2},...}^\infty \br{n}{\beta}=\br{\prodez n}{\prodez 2\beta}=\br{(2\pi)^{1/2}}{(2\beta)^{-1/2}}=\boxed{2(\beta\pi)^{1/2}}}
