\section*{Problem 1}
For the collapse of a black hole itself, there is no necessity of any black hole to collapse, or disappear, and further no necessity for these black holes to have a collapse that lasts for a large time scale, perhaps billions of years. So the lack of any particular mechanism for the destruction of a black hole does not preclude their existence.\~\\

It just so happens that we do have a known mechanism for the loss of mass of a black hole, Hawking radiation, and it does proceed on a very large time scale, much larger than we are able to measure. Hawking radiation works based on the Unruh effect, so we should explain that first. The Unruh effect is the effect that accelerating observers see a thermal bath of photons being emitted from the horizon of the black hole. This thermal bath of photons continues to exist for observers far away from the black hole. This emission of radiation will eventually reduce the mass of the black hole, and eventually cause the black hole to collapse. It is true, however, that it takes a very long time for a black hole to evaporate due to Hawking radiation, about $2\zehnein{100}{years}$.

\section*{Problem 4.c.}
We would like to write the Schwarzschild metric
\g{ds^2=-(1-\br{2}{r})dt^2+(1-\br{2}{r})^{-1}dr^2}
in terms of the Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates, ignoring the $\theta$ and $\phi$ coordinates. Starting with $r<2$,
\g{u=(\br{r}{2}-1)^{1/2}e^{r/4}\cosh(\br{t}{4})}
\g{v=(\br{r}{2}-1)^{1/2}e^{r/4}\sinh(\br{t}{4})}
Find the infinitesimals $du$ and $dv$
\g{du=(\br{r}{2}-1)^{1/2}e^{r/4}\br{1}{4}\sinh(\br{t}{4})dt+(\br{r}{2}-1)^{1/2}e^{r/4}\br{1}{4}dr\cosh(\br{t}{4}+\br{1}{4}(\br{r}{2}-1)^{-1/2}dr e^{r/4}\cosh(\br{t}{4})}
The infinitesimal has three terms, but two of them can be combined noting that,
\g{(\br{r}{2}-1)^{1/2}+(\br{r}{2}-1)^{-1/2}=\br{(\br{r}{2}-1)^{1/2}}{1-\br{2}{r}}}
So $du$ is
\g{du=(\br{r}{2}-1)^{1/2}e^{r/4}\br{1}{4}\sinh(\br{t}{4})dt+\br{1}{1-\br{2}{r}}\cosh(\br{t}{4})dr}
$dv$ swaps the hyperbolic sine functions.
\g{dv=(\br{r}{2}-1)^{1/2}e^{r/4}\br{1}{4}\cosh(\br{t}{4})dt+\br{1}{1-\br{2}{r}}\sinh(\br{t}{4})dr}
We would like to find $ds^2_{\textrm{Schwarzschild}}$ using $du$ and $dv$. The most obvious way to simplify is to take the difference of the squares, $dv^2-du^2$, applying the identity
\g{\cosh^2-\sinh^2=1}
So square $du$ and square $dv$, and take their difference. The result is
\g{dv^2-du^2=(\br{r}{2}-1)e^{r/2}\br{1}{16}(dt^2-(1-\br{2}{r})^{-2}dr^2)}
The parenthesis is almost the Schwarzschild metric. We would like the terms in the parenthesis to be exactly equal to the Schwarzschild metric. So multiply by the necessary factor
\g{\br{(1-\br{2}{r})}{(1-\br{2}{r})}}
which it perfectly aligns the $dt^2$ term and cancels the extra power in the $dr^2$ term, so the $dr^2$ term is also in the form of the Schwarzschild metric.
\g{dv^2-du^2&=\br{(\br{r}{2}-1)}{(1-\br{2}{r})}e^{r/2}\br{1}{16}((1-\br{2}{r})dt^2-(1-\br{2}{r})^{-1}dr^2)
\intertext{The factor on front is $\br{r}{2}$}
&= e^{r/2}\br{2}{32}((1-\br{2}{r})dt^2-(1-\br{2}{r})^{-1}dr^2)}
And so the Schwarzschild interval in Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates is
\g{\boxed{ds^2_{\textrm{Schwarzschild}}=\br{32}{r}e^{-r/2}(du^2-dv^2)}}
The result is identical for $r<2$ and $r>2$.
