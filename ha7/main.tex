\section*{Problem 1.a.}
The kinematics of the relevant $e^{-}p$ collision are described in Thomson p. 168. An electron with $p_1^\mu$ emits a photon with $q^\mu$, and scatters into $p_3^\mu$ at angle $\theta$. The proton with $p_2^\mu$ absorbs the photon and scatters to $p_4^\mu$. The momentum 4-vectors are
\e{p_1=(E_1,0,0,E_1)\quad p_2=(m_p,0,0,0) \quad p_3=(E_3,E_3\sin(\theta), 0,E_3\cos(\theta)) \quad p_4=(E_4,\vec{p}_4)}
\e{q=(E_1-E_3,\vec{p}_1-\vec{p}_3)}
Inelasticity is defined as
\e{y=\fr{p_2 q}{p_2 p_1}}
which results in
\e{\lab{8nas}y=\fr{m_p(E_1-E_3)}{m_p E_1}=\boxed{1-\fr{E_3}{E_1}}}
Expand $Q^2$,
\e{\lab{1283nz}Q^2=-q^2 = -(p_1-p_3)^2=-p_1^2-p_3^2+2p_1p_3=-m_e^2-m_e^2+2E_1E_3(1-\cos(\theta))=\boxed{4E_1E_3 \sin^2(\fr{1}{2}\theta)}}
Next, search for $Q^2=2m_pE_1y$. Start with
\e{qp_2 = (p_1-p_3)p_2 = m_p(E_1-E_3)}
\e{q=p_4-p_2}
\e{p_4^2=(q+p_2)^2=q^2+p_2^2+2qp_2}
\e{p_2^2=p_4^2}
\e{qp_2=-\fr{1}{2}q^2}
\e{\lab{n1823}E_1-E_3=\fr{qp_2}{m_p}=-\fr{q^2}{2m_p}=\fr{Q^2}{2m_p}}
Use \eref{n1823},
\e{\lab{n19283n}Q^2=2m_p (E_1-E_3)=2m_p E_1 \fr{m_p(E_1-E_3)}{m_p E_1}=\boxed{2m_pE_1y}}

\section*{Problem 1.d.}
The solution follows Thomson p. 77 closely. Start with $\fr{d\sigma}{d\Omega}$, solve for the relevant differentials, and then solve for $\fr{d\sigma}{dQ^2}$. Using Azimuthal symmetry, $\fr{dQ^2}{d\Omega}$ is a function of only $\cos(\theta)$, not also $\phi$.
\e{\fr{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\fr{d\sigma}{dQ^2}|\fr{dQ^2}{d\Omega}|=\fr{1}{2\pi}\fr{dQ^2}{d(\cos(\theta))}\fr{d\sigma}{dQ^2}}
Solve for the relevant differentials.
\e{\fr{dQ^2}{d(\cos(\theta))}=2m_p \fr{d E_3}{d(\cos(\theta))}}
\e{\fr{d}{d(\cos(\theta))}E_3=\fr{E_1^2 m_p}{m_p + E_1(1-\cos(\theta))}=\fr{E_3^2}{m_p}}
\e{\fr{d}{d\cos(\theta)}Q^2=2E_3^2}
\e{\fr{d}{d\Omega}\sigma=\fr{1}{2\pi}2E_3^2 \fr{d\sigma}{dQ^2}=\fr{E_3^2}{\pi}\fr{d\sigma}{dQ^2}}
Finally
\e{\lab{n1283n}\boxed{\fr{d\sigma}{dQ^2}=\fr{\pi}{E_3^2} \fr{d\sigma}{d\Omega}}}
\section*{Problem 1.e.}
The Rosenbluth formula is
\e{\fr{d}{d\Omega}\sigma &= \fr{\alpha^2}{4E_1^2\sin^4(\fr{1}{2}\theta)}\fr{E_3}{E_1}(\fr{G_E^2+\tau G_M^2}{1+\tau}\cos^2(\fr{1}{2}\theta)+2\tau G_M^2 \sin^2(\fr{1}{2}\theta))\\
&= \fr{\alpha^2}{4E_1^2\sin^4(\fr{1}{2}\theta)}( \fr{E_3}{E_1} \fr{G_E^2+\tau G_M^2}{1+\tau}\cos^2(\fr{1}{2}\theta)+ \fr{E_3}{E_1} 2\tau G_M^2 \sin^2(\fr{1}{2}\theta))\\
&= A(B+C)}
Thankfully, this equation can be manipulated in terms of $A(B+C)$. Simplify the $B$ term using \eref{n182x3}
\e{B&=\fr{E_3}{E_1}\fr{G_E^2+\tau G_M^2}{1+\tau}\cos^2(\fr{1}{2}\theta)\\
&=\fr{G_E^2+\tau G_M^2}{1+\tau}(1-y-\fr{m_p^2}{Q^2}y^2)}
which is the desired form for the $B$ term. Simplify the $C$ term using \eref{n1283n}, \eref{n19283n}, and \eref{1283nz}.
\e{C&=2\tau G_M^2 \sin^2(\fr{1}{2}\theta)\\
&= \fr{E_3}{E_1}\fr{2Q^2}{4m_p^2}G_M^2 \sin^2(\fr{1}{2}\theta)\\
&= \fr{E_3}{m_p}y G_M^2 \sin^2(\fr{1}{2}\theta) \\
&= \fr{E_3 y G_M^2}{2 m_p}(1-\cos(\theta))\\
&= \fr{1}{2}y^2 G_M^2}
which is the desired form for the $C$ term. Simplify the $A$ term.
\e{A&=\fr{\alpha^2}{4E_1^2\sin^4(\fr{1}{2}\theta)}\\
&= \fr{\alpha^2}{E_1^2 (1-\cos(\theta))^2}\\
&= \fr{4\alpha^2 E_3^2}{Q^4}}
which is the desired form for the $A$ term. Finally use \eref{n1283n} to rewrite the cross section differential in $Q^2$ instead of $\Omega$, which simply introduces a factor of $\fr{\pi}{E_3^2}$.
\e{\fr{d}{dQ^2}\sigma = \fr{\pi}{E_3^2} \fr{4\alpha^2 E_3^2}{Q^4} (\fr{G_E^2+\tau G_M^2}{1+\tau}(1-y-\fr{m_p^2}{Q^2}y^2) + \fr{1}{2}y^2 G_M^2)\\
&=\boxed{\fr{\pi}{E_3^2} \fr{4 \pi \alpha^2}{Q^4} (\fr{G_E^2+\tau G_M^2}{1+\tau}(1-y-\fr{m_p^2 y^2}{Q^2}) + \fr{1}{2}y^2 G_M^2)}}
