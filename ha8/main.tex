\section*{Problem 2.a.}
We would like to solve for $\theta(t)$ for the star-black-hole system. Let $m$ be the mass of the star, and $M$ the mass of the black hole. The angular momentum of the star orbiting the black hole is
\g{L=mvr}
The total energy of the star in a stable circular orbit is
\g{E=\br{1}{2}mv^2-\br{GMm}{r}=0}
Solve for the velocity of the star
\g{v=(\br{2GM}{r})^{1/2}}
Plug into angular momentum,
\g{L=r(\br{2GM}{r})^{1/2}m=(GMp)^{1/2}m}
The angular momentum of the system is conserved. Use it to solve for the angle $\theta(t)$
\g{I=mr^2}
\g{L=I\omega=mr^2\dot{\theta}}
\g{r^2\dot{\theta}^2=(GMp)^{1/2}}
The circular orbit has eccentricity
\g{e=1}
and $r(\theta)$,
\g{\zeich{n8cn}r(\theta)=\br{p}{1+\cos(\theta)}}
Solve for $\dot{\theta}$ and simplify
\g{\dot{\theta}&=(GMp)^{1/2}\br{1}{r^2}\\
&= (\br{GM}{p^3})^{1/2} (1+\cos(\theta))^2}
Separate into a first order differential equation and solve.
\g{\zeich{ansd8n}\int_0^\theta \br{d\theta}{(1+\cos(\theta))^2}=\int_0^t (\br{GM}{p^3})^{1/2} dt}
The $\theta$ integral has analytical solution
\g{\int_0^\theta \br{d\theta}{(1+\cos(\theta))^2}=\br{1}{6}\tan(\br{\theta}{2})^3+\br{1}{2}\tan(\br{\theta}{2})}
\gz{ansd8n} has the form of a polynomial where $x=\tan(\br{\theta}{2})$
\g{\br{1}{6}x^3+\br{1}{2}x+c=0}
Solve the polynomial, and substitute $x$. This solves for the polar angle $\theta$ as a function of $t$.
\g{\boxed{\theta=2\arctan \br{((9c^2+1)^{1/2}-3c)^{2/3}-1}{((9c^2+1)^{1/2}-3c)^{1/3}} \qquad c=-(\br{GM}{p^3})^{1/2}t}}
\section*{Problem 2.b.}
The calculation of the traceless quadrupole moment $Q_{ij}$ proceeds as 
\begin{enumerate}
\item Write the system's Newtonain potential as
\g{\Phi=\int \br{\rho(x')}{|x-x'|}dx'^2}
\item Expand the potential
\g{\br{1}{|x-x'|}=\br{1}{r}+\br{x^jx^{j'}}{r^3}}
\item Integrate the potential to order $x^2$ to obtain the quadrupole moment $I_{ij}$
\g{\Phi(x')=-\br{M}{r}-\br{3 I_{jk} x^j x^k}{2r^5}+...}
\g{M=\int \rho dV_x}
\item Calculate the quadrupole moment
\g{I_{ij}=\int d^3 x \rho (r_i r_j - \br{1}{3}r^3 \delta_{ij}}
\item Using the quadrupole moment calculate the trace free quadrupole moment
\g{P_{ij}=n_i m_j - \delta_{ij}}
\g{Q_{ij}=P^{il} I_{lm} p^{im}}
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Problem 3.c.}
This is a binary merger with two approximately equal masses $m_1$ and $m_2$, circular orbit, and semimajor axis $r$. Kepler's third law in this case is
\g{T=(\br{4\pi^2}{G M}{r^3})^{1/2}}
where $M$ is the combined mass of the black holes and $r$ is the semimajor axis, in other words radius about the center of mass. For the final seconds of the merger binary merger, the orbit is approximately circular,
\g{L_{GW}=\br{32c^5}{5G}(\br{\mu}{M})^2(\br{\pi G M f}{c^3})^{10/3}}
where $f=f_{GW}$, $\mu$ is the reduced mass and $M$ is the sum of the individual black hole masses. We need one more piece of information which is the relationship between the semimajor axis and the time duration of the merger,
\g{r=bt^{1/4}}
where $b$ is some constant of proportionality in units of $ms^{-1/4}$. Solve for $F_{GW}$ in terms of $t$,
\g{L_{GW}&=\br{32c^5}{5G}(\br{\mu}{M})^2(\br{\pi G M f}{c^3})^{10/3}(\br{4\pi}{GM}b^3 t^{3/4})^{-1/2\cdot 10/3}\\
&=\zeich{n8xnc}\boxed{\br{32c^5}{5G}(\br{\mu}{M})^2(\br{\pi G M f}{c^3})^{10/3}(\br{4\pi}{GM})^{-5/3}b^3 t^{-5/4}}}
So \fbox{$L_{GW}$ is proportional to $t^{-5/4}$.} Finally the question asks to compare $L_{GW}$ from 3.b. with the quoted amount of energy loss $E$,
\g{E=3m_\odot = 5.36\zehnein{47}{J}}
There are a few ways to calculate $E$. The simplest way is assuming $L_{GW}$ is constant over the duration of the merger, $\Delta t=150\ein{ms}$.
\g{E_{GW}=L_{GW} \Delta t = 2.12\zehnein{47}{J}}
Which is already fairly agreeable. Another way is to use \gz{n8xnc} and assume some constant of proportionality $b$. We would need some solution which relates the semimajor axis $r$ of the decaying orbit and the time elapsed of the merger, $r(t)=b t^{-1/4}$ which we will decide to not solve for. Another way to solve this is assuming that $L_{GW}=L_{GW0}$ is a constant of proportionality in the function $L_{GW}=L_{GW0} t^{-5/4}$, and integrating over $t$. This diverges as $t$ goes to 0, so we need to decide on when it is physically appropriate to cut off the integral. A simple choice is when the Schwarzschild radii are overlapping. We can conject that the radiation is maximal when the radi are touching, and so we should integrate from the start of the merger until the maximum. By looking at the chirp,
\fig{8cm}{1.png}{Chirp.}
we can see that the peak is about 10 milliseconds separated from the end of the merger. Assuming the constant of proportionality $b$ is indeed 1, the $t$ integral will contribute a factor of
\g{\int_10^200 t^{-5/4}=1.18}
to $L_{GW}$, and so this calculation brings $E$ to
\g{E=2.50\zehnein{47}J}
slightly closer to the quoted value.

\section*{Problem 4.d.}
For this part we desire to calculate the number of photons that could have produced that force. The photons are at wavelength 1000 nm, about that of Nd:YAG lasers employed at LIGO. The amount of momentum transfer for such a force over that amount of time is
\g{p=Ft=FT=3.2\zehnein{-14}{kg m/s}}
Some number of photons must produce that value of momentum transfer $p$. A single photon has momentum 
\g{p=\br{h}{\lambda}}
which can impact on the mirror, be absorbed, and then reemitted, producing another amount of momentum transfer
\g{p=\br{h}{\lambda}}
And so one collision-emission event produces momentum transfer
\g{p_1 = \br{2h}{\lambda} = 1.325\zehnein{-27}{kg m/s}}
The number of total collision-emission events to produce the total momentum transfer $p$ must then be
\g{\br{p}{p_1}=2.415\zehn{13}}
About \fbox{20 trillion photons} at wavelength 1000 nm must collide with the mirror during the period $T$ of the gravitational wave in order to produce the experimentally measured displacement $\delta_x$ of the LIGO mirrors. However the cavity reflects photons back to the mirrors repeatedly, so
\g{\br{p}{\Delta p} \br{1}{\rom{round trips}}=6.44 \zehn{10}}
About \fbox{60 billion photons} at wavelength 1000 nm must be in the cavity during the period $T$ of the gravitational wave in order to produce the experimentally measured displacement $\delta_x$ of the LIGO mirrors.
