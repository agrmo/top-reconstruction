\subsection*{Navier Stokes Equations in Rectangular Coordinates}
Newton's second law is
\e{\vec{F}&=\dd{t}\vec{P}\\
&= dm \fr{D}{Dt}\vec{V}\\
&= dm (u\pa{x} \vec{V} + v\pa{y} \vec{V} + w\pa{z} \vec{V} + \pa{t}\vec{V})}
where $\vec{P}$ is the linear momentum of the system.
\e{\vec{P}=\int_M \vec{V} dm}
where $dm$ is the infinitesimal fluid particle of mass $dm$, and $V$ is the particle velcoity in the fluid.
\e{d\vec{F}=dm \dd{t}\vec{V}}
\e{\rho \fr{D}{Dt}u = \rho g_x - \pa{x} p + \pa{x}(\mu (2\pa{x} u - \fr{2}{3}\nabla \cdot \vec{V})) + \pa{y}(\mu (\pa{y} u + \pa{x} v)) + \pa{z}(\mu (\pa{x} w + \pa{z} u))}
\e{\rho \fr{D}{Dt}v = \rho g_y - \pa{y} p + \pa{x}(\mu (\pa{y} u + \pa{x} v)) + \pa{y}(\mu (2\pa{y} v - \fr{2}{3}\nabla \cdot \vec{V})) + \pa{z}(\mu (\pa{z} v + \pa{y} w))}
\e{\rho \fr{D}{Dt}w = \rho g_z - \pa{z} p + \pa{x}(\mu (\pa{x} w + \pa{z} u)) + \pa{y}(\mu (\pa{z}w+\pa{y}w)) + \pa{z}(\mu (2\pa{z} w - \fr{2}{3}\nabla \cdot \vec{V}))}
For incompressible flow,
\e{\nabla \cdot \vec{V}=0}
For non-convective flow,
\e{\fr{D}{Dt}\vec{V}=(\vec{V}\cdot \nabla)\vec{V}+\pa{t}\vec{V}=\pa{t}\vec{V}}
Assuming incompressible and non-convective flow, the Navier-Stokes equations are,
\e{\rho(\pa{t}u + u\pa{x}u + v\pa{y}u + w\pa{z}u)=\rho g_x - \pa{x}p + \mu(\ppa{x}{2}u + \ppa{y}{2}u + \ppa{z}{2}u)}
\e{\rho(\pa{t}v + u\pa{x}v + v\pa{y}v + w\pa{z}v)=\rho g_y - \pa{y}p + \mu(\ppa{x}{2}v + \ppa{y}{2}v + \ppa{z}{2}v)}
\e{\rho(\pa{t}w + u\pa{x}w + w\pa{y}w + w\pa{z}w)=\rho g_z - \pa{z}p + \mu(\ppa{x}{2}w + \ppa{y}{2}w + \ppa{z}{2}w)}
For frictionless flow,
\e{\mu=0}
Assuming incompressible, non-convective, and frictionless flow, the Navier-Stokes equations are
\e{\rho(\pa{t}u + u\pa{x}u + v\pa{y}u + w\pa{z}u)=\rho g_x - \pa{x}p}
\e{\rho(\pa{t}v + u\pa{x}v + v\pa{y}v + w\pa{z}v)=\rho g_y - \pa{y}p}
\e{\rho(\pa{t}w + u\pa{x}w + w\pa{y}w + w\pa{z}w)=\rho g_z - \pa{z}p}
which can be written in vector form, $\vec{V}=(u,v,w)$, $\vec{g}=(g_x,g_y,g_z)$
\e{\rho(\pa{t}w + u\pa{x}\vec{V} + w\pa{y}\vec{V} + w\pa{z}\vec{V})=\rho \vec{g}-\nabla p}
This is the Euler equation.
\subsection{Navier Stokes Equations in Radial Coordinates}
Assume some fluid exists. Let $v(r,t)$ be the velocity field of the fluid, $M$ be the total mass of the fluid, $V$ the volume, $d \tau$ the infinitestimal volume element. Let $\delta v$ infinitesimal change in velocity per infinitesimal change in time $\delta t$, $dF$ the infinitesimal change in force per infinitesimal change in time $\delta t$. The Newtonian postulate is
\e{dm \delta v=dF \delta t}
\e{M=\int_V \mu d\tau}
The conservation of mass is
\e{\pa{t}\mu + \pa{r} (\mu v)=0}
where $t$ is time, $\mu$ is mass density, and $v$ is the velocity of the fluid. Let $P$ be the stress tensor of the fluid,
\e{P&=-\mathcal{I}p=-\delta{i,k}p\\
&= -Ip(\mu) + \hat{P}(v)\\
&= -Ip(\mu) + (\eta' - 2\eta)I Sp \epsilon}
where $\epsilon$ is the deformation tensor of the fluid,
Differentiate $P$,
\e{\pa{r}P=-\pa{r}Ip=-I\pa{r}p=-\pa{r}p}
This needs to be simplified. Use the ideal gas equation,
\e{pV&=NrT\\
p&=\mu r T\\
dp=d\mu r T}
which gives a relationship between pressure and mass density.
The equations of motion of the fluid is
\e{\mu(\pa{t}+v\pa{r})v&=f+\pa{r}P\\
&= -\mu \pa{r}U-\pa{r}p-\eta \pa{r}\times (\pa{r}\times v)+\eta' \fr{\p^2}{\p r^2}v}
\e{\pa{r}\hat{P}=-\eta \pa{r}\times (\pa{r}\times v)+\eta' \pa{r} \cdot \pa{r}v = -2\eta \pa{r}\times \vec{\omega} + \eta' \pa{r} \xi}
where $\vec{\omega}$ is the rotational friction, and $\xi$ the compression friction,  $\eta$ and $\eta'$ are the coefficients of internal rotational friction and $\eta'$ is the internal compression friction of the fluid. Dividing through by $\mu$ and using 
\e{v\times(\pa{r}\times v)&= \pa{r}\cdot \fr{1}{2}v^2-v\pa{r}\cdot v}
gives the Navier-Stokes equations,
\e{\pa{t}v=-\pa{r}(\fr{1}{2}v^2 + U + \int_V \fr{1}{\mu(p)}dp) + (v-\fr{\eta}{\mu}\pa{r})\times(\pa{r}\times v)+\fr{\eta'}{\mu}\pa{r}\cdot \pa{r}v}
