\section*{Problem 1.a.}
The kinematics of the relevant $e^{-}p$ collision are described in Thomson p. 168. An electron with $p_1^\mu$ emits a photon with $q^\mu$, and scatters into $p_3^\mu$ at angle $\theta$. The proton with $p_2^\mu$ absorbs the photon and scatters to $p_4^\mu$. The momentum 4-vectors are
\e{p_1=(E_1,0,0,E_1)\quad p_2=(m_p,0,0,0) \quad p_3=(E_3,E_3\sin(\theta), 0,E_3\cos(\theta)) \quad p_4=(E_4,\vec{p}_4)}
\e{q=(E_1-E_3,\vec{p}_1-\vec{p}_3)}
Inelasticity is defined as
\e{y=\fr{p_2 q}{p_2 p_1}}
which results in
\e{\lab{8nas}y=\fr{m_p(E_1-E_3)}{m_p E_1}=\boxed{1-\fr{E_3}{E_1}}}
Next, find the four-momentum transfer $Q^2$.
\e{\lab{1283nz}Q^2=-q^2 = -(p_1-p_3)^2=-p_1^2-p_3^2+2p_1p_3=-m_e^2-m_e^2+2E_1E_3(1-\cos(\theta))=\boxed{4E_1E_3\sin^2(\fr{\theta}{2})}}
Next,
\e{qp_2 = (p_1-p_3)p_2 = m_p(E_1-E_3)}
\e{q=p_4-p_2}
\e{p_4^2=(q+p_2)^2=q^2+p_2^2+2qp_2}
\e{p_2^2=p_4^2}
\e{qp_2=-\fr{1}{2}q^2}
\e{\lab{n1823}E_1-E_3=\fr{qp_2}{m_p}=-\fr{q^2}{2m_p}=\fr{Q^2}{2m_p}}
Use \eref{n1823},
\e{\lab{n19283n}Q^2=2m_p (E_1-E_3)=2m_p E_1 \fr{m_p(E_1-E_3)}{m_p E_1}=\boxed{2m_pE_1y}}
Using the kinematics of elastic $e^{-}p$ scattering we find the answers.

\section*{Problem 2.d.}
The two essential equations are \eref{1283nz} and \eref{xn1283n}. We are given
\e{x=0.2\quad Q^2=2\un{GeV}^2 \quad E_3<E_1 \quad E_1<20\un{GeV}}
and asked to find the possible values of $\theta$.
\e{E_1-E_3=\fr{2\un{GeV}^2}{2(0.2)m_p}=5.33\un{GeV}}
The difference between $E_1$ and $E_3$ is constant. The electron necessarily loses 5.33 GeV. Next,
\e{\fr{2\un{GeV}^2}{4E_1E_3}&=\sin^2(\fr{\theta}{2})=\fr{1}{2}(1-\cos(\theta))=\fr{1}{2}-\fr{1}{2}\cos(\theta)\\
\cos(\theta)&=1-\fr{4\un{GeV}^2}{4E_1E_3}\\
\theta&=\arccos(1-\fr{1\un{GeV}^2}{E_1E_3})}
This is the scattering angle $\theta$. We could plot this given the kinematic constraints on $E_1$, $E_3$ but the intuition is adequate: $\arccos(x)$ is maximized as $x\rightarrow -1$, and minimized as $x\rightarrow 1$. $\arccos(1-x)$ is maximized as $x\rightarrow 2$, and minimized as $x\rightarrow 0$. $\arccos(1-\fr{1}{x})$ is maximized as $x\rightarrow0$, and minimized as $x\rightarrow \infty$. Then $\theta$ is maximized as $E_1E_3 \rightarrow 0$, and minimized as $E_1E_3\rightarrow \infty$. The lowest value for the product $E_1E_3$ is simply \fbox{$E_3=2\un{GeV}$} and then necessarily \fbox{$E_1=2+5.33\un{GeV}=7.33\un{GeV}$}. This results in the angle
\e{\boxed{\theta_\rom{max}=\arccos(1-\fr{1\un{GeV}^2}{(7.33\un{GeV})(2\un{GeV})})=21.3^\circ}}
The maximum value for the product $E_1E_3$ is $E_1=20\un{GeV}$ and then necessarily $E_3=20\un{GeV}-5.33\un{GeV}=14.67\un{GeV}$.
\e{\boxed{\theta_\rom{min}=\arccos(1-\fr{1\un{GeV}^2}{(20\un{GeV})(14.67\un{GeV})})=4.73^\circ}}
Using the kinematics of deep inelastic $e^{-}p$ scattering and the given constraints, we find the possible range of the electron scattering angle $\theta$.
\section*{Problem 3.}
This is elastic $e^{-}p$ scattering. The proton propagates from $p_2$ to $p_4$, and the electron propagates from $p_1$ to $p_3$, and the photon travels from the electron to proton with $q$. 
\e{p_4=q+p_2\quad p_2q=\fr{1}{2}q^2 \quad p_2^2=(m_p c)^2}
The spin averaged amplitude is
\e{\langle \abs{M}^2 \rangle = \fr{g_e^4}{q^4}L_e^{\mu\nu}L_{\mu\nu p}}
where
\e{L_e^{\mu\nu}=2(p_1^\mu p_3^\nu+p_1^\nu p_3^\mu+g^{\mu\nu}((m_p c^2)^2-(p_1p_3)))}
and by assuming the proton is point-like,
\e{L^{\mu\nu}_p&=\tr(\gamma^\mu (\bcancel{p}_3+m_p c)\gamma^\nu(\bcancel{p}_4+m_pc))\\
&=4(p^\mu_2p_4^\nu+p_4^\mu p_2^\nu+g^{\mu\nu}((m_pc)^2-(p_2p_4)))\\
&= 2(p_2^\mu(q^\mu-p^\nu)+p_2^\nu(q^\mu+p^\mu)+g^{\mu\nu}((m_p c)^2-p_2(qp_2)))\\
&= 2(p_2^\mu q^\nu + p_2^\nu q^\mu +2p_2^\mu q^\nu + g^{\mu\nu}((m_p c)^2+\fr{1}{2}q^2 - (m_p c)^2))\\
&=\lab{nzlur1a} 2(p_2^\mu q^\nu+p_2^\nu) + 4p_2^\mu p_2^\nu + g^{\mu\nu}q^2}
\eref{nzlur1a} is derived from the spin averaged amplitude and assuming the proton is point-like. If the proton is not assumed to be point-like, the spin averaged amplitude is
\e{\langle \abs{M}^2 \rangle = \fr{g_e^4}{q^4}L_e^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu p}}
where $K^{\mu \nu}$ is an unknown quantity. By Griffiths' line of arguments, we arrive this form of $K^{\mu\nu}$ in (8.18),
\e{K^{\mu\nu}=K_1(-g^{\mu\nu}+\fr{q^\mu q^\nu}{q^2})+\fr{K_2}{(m_p c)^2}(p_2^\mu + \fr{1}{2}q^\mu)(p_2^\nu + \fr{1}{2}q^\nu)}

\lab{n1h283n}\alpha_s(\mu^2)&=\fr{12\pi}{11n-2f}\fr{1}{\ln(\fr{\mu^2}{\Lambda^2})}}
The strong coupling constant $\alpha_s(\abs{q^2})$ is \eref{n1283b} and its inverse $\fr{1}{\alpha_s(\abs{q^2})}$ is \eref{nc71z}. Plug in \eref{n1h283n} into \eref{nc71z}.
\e{\fr{1}{\alpha_s(\abs{q^2})}&=\fr{1}{\alpha_s(\mu^2)}+\fr{11n-2f}{12\pi}\ln(\fr{\abs{q^2}}{\mu^2})\\
&=\lab{1823b123}\fr{11n-2f}{12\pi}\ln(\fr{\mu^2}{\Lambda^2})+\fr{11n-2f}{12\pi}\ln({\abs{q^2}}{\mu^2})}
Invert \eref{1823b123}.
\e{\lab{nashbdzkgq}\boxed{\alpha_s(\abs{q^2})=\fr{12\pi}{11n-2f}\fr{1}{\ln(\fr{\abs{q^2}}{\Lambda^2})}}}
By using the new definition $\ln(\Lambda^2)$ and plugging it into the strong coupling constant $\alpha_s(\abs{q^2})$, we find the strong coupling constant is now a function of $\abs{q^2}$ and $\Lambda^2$ only.
\section*{Problem 7.}
The strong coupling constant is
\e{\lab{n128al}\alpha_s(q^2)=\fr{\alpha_s(\mu^2)}{1+\fr{11N_c-2N_f}{12\pi}\alpha_s(\mu^2)\ln(\fr{q^2}{\mu^2})}}
There are a couple ways to answer this question: 1) assuming a scale $\mu^2$, 2) ignoring the scale $\mu^2$, and 3) using the scale $\Lambda$. This answer will show all three answers and discuss them. For answer \#1, \eref{n128al} clearly diverges at
\e{1&=-\fr{11N_c-2N_f}{12\pi}\alpha_s(\mu^2)\ln(\fr{q^2}{\mu^2})\\
&=\fr{2N_f-11N_c}{12\pi}\alpha_s(\mu^2)\ln(\fr{q^2}{\mu^2})\\
\fr{12\pi}{(2N_f-11N_c)\alpha_s(\mu^2)}&=\ln(\fr{q^2}{\mu^2})}
\e{\boxed{q^2=\mu^2 e^{\fr{12\pi}{(2N_f-11N_c)\alpha_s(\mu^2)}}}}
In this answer, $\alpha_s(q^2)$ diverges at this value of $q^2$, which depends on $\alpha_s(\mu^2)$, $\mu^2$, $N_f$, and $N_c$. We can estimate a value of $q$ by using data at the $Z$ peak, where $N_f=3$, $N_c=6$, $\mu^2=m_Z^2$, and $\alpha_s(\mu^2)=0.1184\pm 0.0007$ as given in Thomson. Then
\e{q=((91.18\un{GeV})^2 e^{\fr{12\pi}{(6-33)0.1184}})^{\fr{1}{2}}=\boxed{250\un{MeV}}}
This is indeed within the range of experimental values for $\Lambda$. Answer \#2 does not assume a scale $\mu^2$. Invert \eref{n128al}.
\e{\fr{1}{\alpha_s(q^2)}=\fr{1}{\alpha_s(\mu^2)}+\fr{11N_c-2N_f}{12\pi}\ln(\fr{q^2}{\mu^2})}
$\alpha_s(\mu^2)$ is the measured value of the coupling at some scale $\mu^2$, and is constant, so $\fr{1}{\alpha_s(\mu^2)}$ is also a constant. We are interested in the divergences of $\alpha_s(q^2)$ so drop this term.
\e{\fr{1}{\alpha_s(q^2)}&=-\fr{11N_c-2N_f}{12\pi}\ln(\fr{q^2}{\mu^2})}
Invert.
\e{\lab{1n283nz}\alpha_s(q^2)&=-\fr{12\pi}{11N_c-2N_f}\fr{1}{\ln(\fr{q^2}{\mu^2})}}
\eref{1n283nz} diverges when
\e{\lim_{q^2\rightarrow \mu^2} \fr{1}{\ln(\fr{q^2}{\mu^2})}=\infty}
\e{\boxed{\lim_{q^2\rightarrow \mu^2} \alpha_s(q^2)=\infty}}
As $q^2$ approaches the scale $\mu^2$, the coupling $\alpha_s(q^2)$ diverges. This is one possible answer to the question, where $\alpha_s(\mu^2)$ is dropped. Finally for answer \#3, use \eref{nashbdzkgq} which parameterizes $\alpha_s$ in terms of $\Lambda$. 
\e{\alpha_s(\abs{q^2})=\fr{12\pi}{11n-2f}\fr{1}{\ln(\fr{\abs{q^2}}{\Lambda^2})}}
In this case, the divergence is more clear.
\e{\ln(\fr{|q^2|}{\Lambda^2})=0}
\e{\fr{|q^2|}{\Lambda^2}=1}
\e{\boxed{|q^2|=\Lambda^2}}
The divergence of $\alpha_s$ happens at the scale $|q|=\Lambda$. Griffiths cites that $100\un{MeV}< \Lambda <500\un{MeV}$. Then \fbox{$\alpha_s$ diverges as $100\un{MeV}<|q|<500 \un{MeV}$}.
