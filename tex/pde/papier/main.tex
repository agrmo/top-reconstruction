The Navier-Stokes equations are derived from Newtonian principles. For a special case a solution is found as a Fourier series.
\section{Navier-Stokes Equations in Rectangular Coordinates}
Consider a three dimensional box enclosing a fluid. The position of the particle in the fluid is given by the solution $u(t,x,y,z)$, whose velocity field is $\vec{V}(x,y,z)$.
\fig{7cm}{1.png}{Three dimensional box with all stresses in the $x$ direction shown as a Taylor expansion. \cite{fox}}
Assume there are two types of forces on the fluid: 1) the force of gravity; 2) the force from the fluid along the boundaries. The force on the fluid by gravity along the $a$ axis is
\e{\rho g_a}
where $\rho$ is the mass density of the volume element. The force on the fluid from the fluid is quantified by the shear stress $\tau_{ab}$: the rate of change in position of the fluid in the $b$ axis, given a force acting in the $y$ axis. Suppose that, for each face of the box, the shear stress $\tau$ is approximated by a Taylor expansion to linear order, so that the shear stress is at most linearly dependent on position. The total force on the fluid along the $a$ axis $dF_a$ the sum of these two contributions. 
\e{dF_x=(\rho g_x + \pa{x}\tau_{xx}+\pa{y}\tau_{yx}+\pa{z}\tau_{zx})dxdydz}
\e{dF_y=(\rho g_y + \pa{x}\tau_{xy}+\pa{y}\tau_{yy}+\pa{z}\tau_{zy})dxdydz}
\e{dF_z=(\rho g_z + \pa{x}\tau_{xz}+\pa{y}\tau_{yz}+\pa{z}\tau_{zz})dxdydz}
This expresses the net force on the particle in all directions. We are now in a position to apply Newton's force equation $\vec{F}=m\vec{a}$. Newton's second law is
\e{\vec{F}&=m\vec{a}\\
&=\dd{t}\vec{P}\\
&= dm (u\pa{x} \vec{V} + v\pa{y} \vec{V} + w\pa{z} \vec{V} + \pa{t}\vec{V})}
where $\vec{P}$ is the linear momentum of the system.
\e{\vec{P}=\int_M \vec{V} dm}
where $dm$ is the infinitesimal fluid particle of mass $dm$, and $V$ is the particle velcoity in the fluid.
\e{d\vec{F}=dm \dd{t}\vec{V}}
We need a way to quantify the acceleration field $\vec{a}$. Use the time derivative on $\vec{V}$ and apply the chain rule.
\e{\vec{a}&=\dd{t}\vec{V}=\pa{t}\vec{V}+\pa{x}\vec{V}\dd{t}x+\pa{y}\vec{V}\dd{t}y+\pa{z}\vec{V}\dd{t}z\\
&=\pa{t}\vec{V}+u\pa{x}\vec{V}+v\pa{y}\vec{V}+w\pa{z}\vec{V}\\
&=\pa{t}\vec{V}+(u\pa{x}+v\pa{y}+w\pa{z})\vec{V}}
This is intuitively explained by two types of contributions: 1) the change in the velocity field $\vec{V}$ per unit time; 2) the change in position of the velocity field $\vec{V}$ by deformation. Now apply $\vec{F}=m\vec{a}$ infinitesimally.
\e{d\vec{F}&=\rho d\vec{a}\\
\lab{infa} \rho g_x + \pa{x}\tau_{xx}+\pa{y}\tau_{yx}+\pa{z}\tau_{zx}&=\rho(\pa{t}u+u\pa{x}u+v\pa{y}u+w\pa{z}u)\\
\lab{infb}\rho g_y + \pa{x}\tau_{xy}+\pa{y}\tau_{yy}+\pa{z}\tau_{zy}&=\rho(\pa{t}v+u\pa{x}v+v\pa{y}v+w\pa{z}v)\\
\lab{infc}\rho g_z + \pa{x}\tau_{xz}+\pa{y}\tau_{yz}+\pa{z}\tau_{zz}&=\rho(\pa{t}w+u\pa{x}w+v\pa{y}w+w\pa{z}w)}
These are the Navier-Stokes equations. They express Newton's force equation $\vec{F}=m\vec{a}$ as a relationship between 1) shear forces by fluid on the boundary; 2) deformation forces by the fluid on the boundary; 3) gravity on the fluid. We need an expression for $\tau$. Not shown here, $\tau$ can be derived from thermodynamic equations for an ideal gas.
\e{\tau_{xy}=\tau_{yx}=\mu(\pa{x}v+\pa{y}u)}
\e{\tau_{yz}=\tau_{zy}=\mu(\pa{y}w+\pa{z}v)}
\e{\tau_{zx}=\tau_{xz}=\mu(\pa{z}u+\pa{x}w)}
\e{\tau_{xx}=-p-\fr{2}{3}\mu \nabla\cdot\vec{V}+2\mu\pa{x}u}
\e{\tau_{yy}=-p-\fr{2}{3}\mu \nabla\cdot\vec{V}+2\mu\pa{y}v}
\e{\tau_{zz}=-p-\fr{2}{3}\mu \nabla\cdot\vec{V}+2\mu\pa{z}w}
Substitute into the Navier-Stokes equations \eref{infa}, \eref{infb}, \eref{infc},
\e{\rho g_x - \pa{x} p + \pa{x}(\mu (2\pa{x} u - \fr{2}{3}\nabla \cdot \vec{V})) + \pa{y}(\mu (\pa{y} u + \pa{x} v)) + \pa{z}(\mu (\pa{x} w + \pa{z} u)) &= \rho(\pa{t}u+u\pa{x}u+v\pa{y}u+w\pa{z}u)\\
\rho g_y - \pa{y} p + \pa{x}(\mu (\pa{y} u + \pa{x} v)) + \pa{y}(\mu (2\pa{y} v - \fr{2}{3}\nabla \cdot \vec{V})) + \pa{z}(\mu (\pa{z} v + \pa{y} w)) &= \rho(\pa{t}v+u\pa{x}v+v\pa{y}v+w\pa{z}v)\\
\rho g_z - \pa{z} p + \pa{x}(\mu (\pa{x} w + \pa{z} u)) + \pa{y}(\mu (\pa{z}w+\pa{y}w)) + \pa{z}(\mu (2\pa{z} w - \fr{2}{3}\nabla \cdot \vec{V})) &= \rho(\pa{t}w+u\pa{x}w+v\pa{y}w+w\pa{z}w)}
These are the Navier-Stokes equations, assuming the fluid is influced by linear shear stresses, linear deformations, gravity, with internal compressions for an ideas gas. We will consider special cases for solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations. For incompressible flow,
\e{\nabla \cdot \vec{V}=0}
For non-convective flow,
\e{\fr{d}{dt}\vec{V}=\pa{t}\vec{V}}
For frictionless flow,
\e{\mu=0}
For gravitationless flow,
\e{g_x=g_y=g_z=0}
Assuming incompressible flow, the Navier-Stokes equations are,
\e{\rho(\pa{t}u + u\pa{x}u + v\pa{y}u + w\pa{z}u)=\rho g_x - \pa{x}p + \mu(\ppa{x}{2}u + \ppa{y}{2}u + \ppa{z}{2}u)}
\e{\rho(\pa{t}v + u\pa{x}v + v\pa{y}v + w\pa{z}v)=\rho g_y - \pa{y}p + \mu(\ppa{x}{2}v + \ppa{y}{2}v + \ppa{z}{2}v)}
\e{\rho(\pa{t}w + u\pa{x}w + w\pa{y}w + w\pa{z}w)=\rho g_z - \pa{z}p + \mu(\ppa{x}{2}w + \ppa{y}{2}w + \ppa{z}{2}w)}
\section{Incompressible, nongravitational, non-convective}
Assuming incompressible, nongravitational, non-convective flow, the Navier-Stokes equations are
\e{0 &= \mu(\ppa{x}{2}u + \ppa{y}{2}u + \ppa{z}{2}u)}
\e{0 &= \mu(\ppa{x}{2}v + \ppa{y}{2}v + \ppa{z}{2}v)}
\e{0 &= \mu(\ppa{x}{2}w + \ppa{y}{2}w + \ppa{z}{2}w)}
Which are three non-coupled Laplace equations in three dimensions. Let's solve the Laplace equation in three dimensions.
\e{\ppa{x_1}{2}u + \ppa{x_2}{2}u + \ppa{x_3}{2}u = 0}
Let the boundary be a box with dimensions $l_1$, $l_2$, $l_3$.
\e{0\leq x_1 \leq l_1 \quad 0\leq x_2 \leq l_2 \quad 0 \leq x_2 \leq l_3}
Let the boundary conditions be vanishing Dirichlet boundary conditions, except one face, with boundary $f(x_1,x_2)$.
\e{u(0,x_2,x_3)=u(l_1,x_2,x_3)=u(x_1,0,x_3)=u(x_1,l_2,x_3)=u(x_1,x_2,0)=0}
\e{u(x_1,x_2,l_3)=f(x_1,x_2)}
Assume the solution is separable, and find three PDEs to solve.
\e{u(x_1,x_2,x_3)=y_1(x_1)y_2(x_2)y_3(x_3)}
Plug into the PDE and rearrange, so that two independent equations. Set them equal to $\lambda$.
\e{\lab{na8sd}\fr{1}{y_1y_2}(\ppa{x_1}{2} (y_1y_2) + \ppa{x_2}{2} (y_1y_2) )=-\fr{1}{y_3}\ppa{x_3}{2}y_3=\lambda}
This gives the PDE for $y_3(x_3)$.
\e{\lab{n82zm}\boxed{\ppa{x_3}{2}y_3(x_3)-\lambda y_3(x_3)=0}}
Simplify \eref{na8sd}.
\e{\fr{1}{y_1(x_1)}\ppa{x_1}{2}y_1(x_1)+\fr{1}{y_2(x_2)}\ppa{x_2}{2}y_2(x_2)=-\lambda}
Rearrange and find two more independent equations. Set them equal to $\mu$.
\e{\lab{N812c}-\lambda-\fr{1}{y_2}\ppa{x_2}{2}y_2 = \fr{1}{y_1}\ppa{x_1}{2}y_1=\mu}
\eref{N812c} gives the PDE for $y_1$.
\e{\boxed{\ppa{x_1}{2} y_1(x_1) + \mu y_1(x_2)=0}}
And similarly \eref{N812c} gives the PDE for $y_2$.
\e{\boxed{\ppa{x_2}{2}y_2(x_2)-(\lambda+\mu)y_2(x_2)=0}}
We want to solve the boxed PDEs to find the general solution $u$. The solution to $y_1$ is
\e{y_1(x_1)=c_1 \sin(\mu^{1/2} x_1) + x_2 \cos(\mu^{1/2} x_1)}
Apply the boundary conditions.
\e{y_1(0)=0 \Rightarrow \boxed{c_2=0}}
\e{\lab{nc81} y_1(l_1)=0 \Rightarrow \mu^{1/2} = \boxed{\pi n_1 l_1^{-1}}}
\e{\boxed{y_1(x_1)=c_1 \sin(\pi n_1 l_1^{-1} x_1)}}
This solves the PDE for $y_1$. The solution for $y_2$ is similar to $y_1$.
\e{y_2(x_2)=c_3 \sin((\lambda+\mu)^{1/2}x_2)+c_4\cos((\lambda+\mu)^{1/2}x_2)}
\e{y_2(0)=0\Rightarrow \boxed{c_4=0}}
\e{\lab{nf812} y_2(l_2)=0 \Rightarrow (\lambda+\mu)^{1/2} = \boxed{\pi n_2 l_2^{-1}}}
\e{\boxed{y_2(x_2)=c_3\sin(\pi n_2 l_2^{-1} x_2)}}
Observe that \eref{nf812} and \eref{nc81} determine $\lambda$, so solve for it now. Observe that $\lambda>0$.
\e{\lambda=(\pi n_2 l_2^{-1})^2 + \mu = (\pi n_1 l_1^{-1})^2 + (\pi n_2 l_2^{-1})^2}
This solves the PDE for $y_2$. Next solve for $y_3$, and put it in hyperbolic form.
\e{r_1,r_2=\pm\sqrt{\lambda}}
\e{y=c_1 e^{r_1 x} + c_2 e^{r_2 x} = c_1 \cosh (r_1 x) + c_2 \cosh(r_2 x)}
\e{y_3(x_3)= c_5 \cosh(\lambda^{1/2} x_3) + c_6 \cosh(\lambda^{1/2} x_3)}
\e{y_3(0)=0\Rightarrow c_6=0}
\e{\boxed{y_3(x_3)=c_3\sinh(\lambda^{1/2}x_3)=c_5 \sinh(((\pi n_1 l_1^{-1})^2 + (\pi n_2 l_2^{-1})^2)^{1/2} x_3)}}
With the solutions for $y_1$, $y_2$, $y_3$, combine them and apply the superposition principle.
\e{u(x_1,x_2,x_3)&=y_1(x_1)y_2(x_2)y_3(x_3)\\
&=\boxed{\sum_{n_1=1}^\infty \sum_{n_2=1}^\infty A(n_1,n_2)\sin(\pi n_1 l_1^{-1} x_1) \sin(\pi l_2^{-1} n_2 x_2) \sinh(((\pi n_1 l_1^{-1})^2 + (\pi n_2 l_2^{-1})^2)^{1/2} x_3)}}
This is the solution for $u(x_1,x_2,x_3)$. Find $A(n_1,n_2)$ using the boundary condition $f(x_1,x_2)$.
\e{\lab{n1239z}u(x_1,x_2,l_3)&=f(x_1,x_2)\\
&=\sum_{n_1=1}^\infty \sum_{n_2=1}^\infty A(n_1,n_2)\sin(\pi n_1 l_1^{-1} x_1) \sin(\pi l_2^{-1} n_2 x_2) \sinh(((\pi n_1 l_1^{-1})^2 + (\pi n_2 l_2^{-1})^2)^{1/2} l_3)}
Rearrange \eref{n1239z} and observe it is a Fourier series expansion for $f$.
\e{A(n_1,n_2)&=\sinh(((\pi n_1 l_1^{-1})^2 + (\pi n_2 l_2^{-1})^2)^{1/2} l_3)^{-1}\\
&\times \int_0^{l_2} \int_0^{l_1} \sin(\pi n_1 l_1^{-1} x_1)^2 \sin(\pi n_2 l_2^{-1} x_2)^2 dx_1 dx_2\\
&\times \int_0^{l_2} \int_0^{l_1} f(x_1,x_2) \sin(\pi n_1 l_1^{-1} x_1) \sin(\pi n_2 l_2^{-1} x_2) dx_1 dx_2
\intertext{The integral over $\sin(...)^2 \sin(...)^2$ evaluates to $\fr{4}{l_1 l_2}$, which was shown earlier in class.}
&= \boxed{\fr{4}{l_1 l_2 \sinh(((\pi n_1 l_1^{-1})^2 + (\pi n_2 l_2^{-1})^2)^{1/2} l_3)} \int_0^{l_2} \int_0^{l_1} f(x_1,x_2) \sin(\pi n_1 l_1^{-1} x_1) \sin(\pi n_2 l_2^{-1} x_2) dx_1 dx_2}}
This is the solution for $A(n_1,n_2)$. Compare this solution with Asmar p. 169 \cite{asm}. For the Navier-Stokes problem, this solution is applicable if the velocity field is zero in the shape of a box, $\vec{V}=(0,0,0)$, except on one face, where $\vec{V}=(f(x_1,x_2),g(x_1,x_2),h(x_1,x_2))$.
\section{Non-gravitational, Non-convective}
For non-gravitational, non-convective flow, but still compressible flow, the Navier-Stokes equations are
\e{\rho \pa{t}u = - \pa{x}p}
\e{\rho \pa{t}v = - \pa{y}p}
\e{\rho \pa{t}w = - \pa{z}p}
Which are three separable one-dimensional ODEs.
\section{Non-gravitational, Non-convective}
For non-gravitational, non-convective, but non-static flow, the Navier-Stokes equations are
\e{\rho \pa{t}u &= \mu(\ppa{x}{2}u + \ppa{y}{2}u + \ppa{z}{2}u)}
\e{\rho \pa{t}v &= \mu(\ppa{x}{2}v + \ppa{y}{2}v + \ppa{z}{2}v)}
\e{\rho \pa{t}w &= \mu(\ppa{x}{2}w + \ppa{y}{2}w + \ppa{z}{2}w)}
These are three non-coupled three dimensional Heat equations.
